# Law, Planning, and Military Urbanism: The Making of Lahore Cantonment, 1849–1947

An archival and historiographical investigation into the administrative, legal, and spatial transformation of colonial Lahore. This research evaluates how British colonial policies reshaped the urban landscape of the subcontinent through the establishment of the Lahore Cantonment.

## Research Abstract & Overview
This project examines the structural evolution of Lahore Cantonment from the onset of British annexation in 1849 up to the partition of 1947. Rather than viewing the cantonment merely as a military outpost, this study investigates it as a complex apparatus of colonial urbanism. By analyzing archival records, legislative frameworks, and town planning strategies, this work uncovers how colonial law and military necessities combined to produce a highly segregated and controlled civic environment, fundamentally altering the spatial history of the Punjab.

## Key Research Themes
* **Colonial Spatiality:** The deliberate design and physical segregation of military zones from the historic local urban fabric.
* **Legal Frameworks:** How municipal bylaws, land acquisition acts, and cantonment codes served as instruments of imperial authority.
* **Historiography of the Punjab:** Re-evaluating British-Indian administrative archives to construct a modern perspective on South Asian urban developments.
